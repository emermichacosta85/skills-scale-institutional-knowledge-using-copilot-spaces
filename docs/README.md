# OctoAcme Project Management Processes

Welcome to the OctoAcme Project Management documentation hub. This directory contains comprehensive guidance for running projects at OctoAcme using our standardized framework.

## Quick Overview

OctoAcme follows a structured lifecycle approach to project delivery with emphasis on:
- **Customer-first** thinking
- **Iterative delivery** of value
- **Clear ownership** and accountability
- **Data-informed** decision-making
- **Psychological safety** for continuous learning

## Project Lifecycle

Our projects follow five key phases:

1. **Initiation** – Validate the problem, align stakeholders, and decide go/no-go
2. **Planning** – Break work into shippable increments with clear timelines
3. **Execution & Tracking** – Deliver daily with visibility and quality gates
4. **Release & Deployment** – Standardize handoff to production with minimal risk
5. **Retrospective & Improvement** – Capture learnings and drive continuous improvement

## Core Roles

- **Project Manager** – Coordinates delivery, schedules, risks, and communications
- **Product Manager** – Defines outcomes, prioritizes, and measures success
- **Developers** – Implement features collaboratively
- **QA/Testing** – Validates quality and acceptance criteria
- **Stakeholders** – Provide inputs and approvals

## Process Overview

OctoAcme's project management approach is built around a clear lifecycle that moves from initiation to planning, execution, release, and retrospective. In the initiation phase, teams validate the business need, define success metrics, align stakeholders, and create a lightweight one-pager that captures the problem, goals, milestones, dependencies, and resource needs. Once approved, the team moves into planning by creating a prioritized backlog, estimating scope, clarifying acceptance criteria, and documenting a definition of done, release plan, and key dependencies. This ensures work is broken into shippable increments and tied to measurable outcomes rather than vague deliverables.

The process emphasizes a set of core roles and shared ownership. Product leads define outcomes and prioritize the roadmap, project managers coordinate delivery, schedules, risks, and communications, and developers, QA, and stakeholders contribute throughout the lifecycle. This role clarity is paired with a strong emphasis on customer value, iterative delivery, data-informed decisions, and a collaborative culture that encourages learning and feedback.

Communication is intentionally regular and structured. The team relies on weekly PM and product alignment, standups or sprint rituals for the delivery team, milestone-based stakeholder updates, and escalation paths for blockers. The documentation also stresses a single source of truth for status, such as the project README or release notes, and encourages teams to track risks, dependencies, and changes in visible project artifacts. For incidents or significant issues, communication includes triage summaries, actions underway, expected timelines, and post-incident retrospectives to support accountability without blame.

Quality assurance is integrated into the workflow rather than treated as a final checkpoint. The project docs require CI checks, unit and integration testing, end-to-end smoke testing for critical flows, security scanning, and manual QA when needed to validate feature acceptance. The lifecycle also includes release and deployment standards, rollback planning, and post-release verification to reduce risk and improve observability. Finally, retrospectives are used after each sprint or milestone to capture what went well, what can improve, and which action items should be tracked through the backlog with owners and due dates.
